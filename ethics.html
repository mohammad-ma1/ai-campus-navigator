AI Ethics

The AI Traffic Light

A simple way to know what's OK, what's risky, and what's a clear no.

GO — Responsible use

Ask AI to explain a concept you don't understand.

Pure learning — you still do the thinking.

Use AI to generate practice questions.

Active recall is one of the best ways to study.

Ask AI to summarize a paper before you read it.

Helps you orient yourself, then read deeply.

Have AI quiz you on a topic.

Self-testing improves long-term memory.

CAUTION — Be careful

Ask AI to improve your draft.

OK if you keep your voice, ideas, and the work is yours. Cite AI assistance if your course requires.

Use AI to debug your code.

Fine for learning. Make sure you understand the fix — don't paste blindly.

Use AI for translation in homework.

Useful, but check accuracy. Some courses see translated answers as borderline.

Use AI to brainstorm ideas for a project.

Great for inspiration. Choose and refine ideas yourself.

STOP — Unethical / not allowed

Submit an AI-generated essay as your own work.

Plagiarism in most universities — even if rephrased.

Use AI during an exam without permission.

Academic dishonesty. Always check exam rules.

Have AI write your final-year thesis.

Defeats the purpose of the degree. High risk of being caught.

Paste a classmate's work into AI without consent.

Privacy violation and possibly academic misconduct.
