LLMs 101

The 10 core ideas every student should know — in plain language, with university examples.

1

LLM (Large Language Model)

A computer program trained on huge amounts of text that learns to predict the next word. That's how it can chat, summarize, and explain.

Example: Like a very well-read study buddy who has skimmed millions of books — helpful, but not always right.
2

Generative AI

AI that creates new content (text, images, code, audio) instead of just classifying or searching.

Example: Asking it to draft an email, generate a diagram idea, or write a poem about Fourier transforms.
3

Chatbot

The conversational interface you talk to. The chatbot is the front door; the LLM is the brain behind it.

Example: ChatGPT is the chatbot, GPT is the underlying model.
4

Prompt

What you type to the AI. Better prompts = better answers.

Example: “Explain TCP handshake to a 2nd-year CS student in 5 bullet points.”
5

Output

What the AI gives back: text, code, an image, etc.

Example: A summary of your lecture notes, or a debugged version of your C function.
6

Tokens

The chunks of text the model reads and writes. Roughly 1 token ≈ ¾ of a word in English.

Example: A 500-word essay is about 700 tokens. Limits on free plans are often counted in tokens.
7

Training

The process of teaching the model by showing it billions of text examples. Training stops at a certain date — that's the “knowledge cutoff”.

Example: If a model was trained until early 2024, it won't know about things from later — unless it can browse the web.
8

Context

Everything the model can “see” at once: your prompt + previous messages + uploaded files. Bigger context = it can handle longer documents.

Example: Pasting a full PDF chapter and asking for a summary uses a lot of context.
9

Multimodal AI

AI that handles more than text — images, audio, video, files.

Example: Snap a photo of a circuit diagram and ask the AI to explain it.
10

Hallucinations

When the AI confidently makes things up — fake citations, wrong formulas, invented facts.

Example: It might invent an author or a paper that doesn't exist. Always verify important claims.
