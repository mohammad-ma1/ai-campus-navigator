Stay safe

Privacy Risk Meter

Before you paste, think. Here's how risky common student situations really are.

Asking for an explanation of a public concept (e.g. Fourier transform)

LOW RISK

Totally fine — no personal data involved.

Pasting your own anonymous lecture notes

LOW RISK

Generally safe. Avoid notes that contain other students' names or grades.

Uploading a photo of your textbook page

LOW RISK

Usually OK for personal study. Don't redistribute copyrighted content.

Pasting code from a class assignment

MEDIUM RISK

Be careful — some courses forbid sharing assignment code. Check your policy.

Asking AI to improve a draft email to your professor

MEDIUM RISK

OK if you remove names, IDs, and anything sensitive about others.

Uploading lecture slides from your university

MEDIUM RISK

Often copyrighted. Acceptable for personal use, risky to publish or share.

Pasting a CV with your full address and phone number

HIGH RISK

Remove personal identifiers first. Use a generic placeholder address.

Sharing a friend's project work without permission

HIGH RISK

Don't share other people's work. Get consent first.

Uploading screenshots that contain your email or login

HIGH RISK

Crop or blur all credentials before uploading anything.

Pasting a personal ID, passport, or student card number

VERY HIGH RISK

Never. This is high-value personal data — keep it offline.

Uploading medical records or mental health information

VERY HIGH RISK

Absolutely not. Even if it's about you. Use professional, private channels.

Sharing unpublished research data from your lab

VERY HIGH RISK

Major academic risk. Always ask your supervisor before using AI on lab data.

The one-question test

Before you paste anything into an AI tool, ask: would I be OK posting this publicly?
